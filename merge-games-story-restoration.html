STORY-DRIVEN MERGE GAMES

Merge Games With Story and Restoration

Updated 5 October 2026

A merge board is easy to understand: create items, combine matches and work toward higher tiers. Story and restoration give that loop a destination by connecting each completed order to characters, locations and visible progress.

Why story matters in a merge game

Story answers a simple question: why am I making these objects? A requested tool may help a character, a completed order may reveal a memory, and a reward may unlock the next part of a location. That context can make ordinary merge decisions feel more purposeful.

Why restoration is a strong progression system

Restoration provides permanent change. The merge board is constantly being rearranged, but a repaired terrace, garden or resort area remains as evidence of progress. This creates a useful contrast between short-term board management and long-term world building.

Orders connect the two systems

Character orders act as the bridge between puzzle and story. They tell players what to produce, create resource-management choices and convert merge-board progress into coins or other rewards that can be spent on restoration.

A good loop has three time scales

Immediate

Merge matching objects and free useful board space.

Session

Finish character orders and collect rewards.

Long term

Restore locations, unlock chapters and discover more of the story.

How Nerelune uses the model

In Nerelune: Secrets of the Cove, the merge-2 board supports character requests, while earned coins are used to restore areas of a forgotten seaside resort. Chapters and memories add a narrative layer so the player is not only upgrading item chains, but also uncovering what happened around the cove.
